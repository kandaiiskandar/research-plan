A declared risk level constrains an AI system only if it changes what the system is permitted to recommend, and its presence in a specification establishes neither that it does so nor how often it binds in operation. We take the governed object to be the admissible recommendation set, apply an operational criterion under which a declared level changes governance behaviour only if it changes that set, and measure how often the change binds rather than how far the set contracts. Four configurations were defined so that a participation-only gate and an intermediate advisory-scope configuration differ at one state only, and were replayed over five years of hourly environmental records for one coastal-fisheries deployment site in Kota Kinabalu, Sabah. The intermediate restriction bound in 5.81% of evaluated departure-window hours under the PRIMARY configuration and 4.48% under RESOLUTION. That frequency is conditional rather than fixed: it spans a deterministic configuration range of 5.67-9.56% across externally sourced threshold settings and falls to 1.96% and 1.26% under medium- and large-vessel configurations. Ocean state accounted for 97.6% of PRIMARY CAUTION classifications. Because historical marine-warning data were unavailable, that component was held at its least-severe value, so these activation frequencies are lower bounds under the declared exclusion. The measurements characterise governance activation rather than safety or decision outcomes. They indicate how a declared governance level can be audited through the recommendation constraints it imposes and the conditions under which those constraints bind.
