**Abstract:** Small-scale coastal fishers make departure decisions under changing environmental conditions while often relying on fragmented sources of information. This study presents a graduated safety-state-gated architecture for AI decision support in low-resource coastal fisheries. The architecture classifies operating conditions into three deterministic states: SAFE, CAUTION, and UNSAFE. SAFE permits full AI advisory support, CAUTION retains AI participation but restricts the available recommendations to {Go, Delay}, and UNSAFE disables AI advice. The architecture was evaluated retrospectively using historical environmental reanalysis. A five-year PRIMARY dataset contained 43,848 hourly reanalysis records, including 9,135 records within a researcher-defined morning evaluation window. Compared with binary gating, graduated gating restricted the admissible advisory scope while retaining AI participation in 5.81% of evaluated records. An alternative environmental-data configuration produced 4.48%. These preliminary results show that the intermediate CAUTION state has a measurable effect on AI advisory availability under historical coastal conditions. The findings characterise governance behaviour and do not establish real-world safety or decision quality.

**Keywords:** AI governance, safety-state gating, decision support, coastal fisheries, advisory scope

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Small-scale coastal fishing operations are influenced by changing environmental and operational conditions, including wind, rainfall, waves, marine warnings, vessel characteristics, and time of day. These factors may need to be considered together when deciding whether and when to depart. For fishers operating with limited decision-support infrastructure, integrating such information into a practical advisory process remains challenging.

Artificial intelligence can support this process by integrating environmental information and generating recommendations. However, the use of AI in safety-related decision support introduces an important governance question: should AI retain the same advisory capability as operating conditions become more restrictive? Safety-critical AI research has highlighted the need for architectural controls and guardrails that constrain system behaviour rather than relying only on unrestricted model outputs [1], [2].

A simple binary gate can determine whether AI is active or inactive. This is useful for preventing AI advice under severe conditions, but it provides limited control over situations in which AI may remain useful while some recommendations should no longer be available. State-dependent restriction of automated or advisory behaviour has precedent in safety-critical systems [3]. The focus of this study is therefore not to claim a new general mechanism for such restriction, but to operationalise graduated advisory control for low-resource coastal fisheries.

The proposed architecture uses three deterministic operating states. Under SAFE, AI may provide its full advisory set. Under CAUTION, AI remains available but its advisory scope is reduced. Under UNSAFE, AI advice is withdrawn and decision authority remains with the human user.

**RQ1:** How can a graduated safety-state-gated architecture constrain AI participation and advisory scope according to classified operating conditions in low-resource coastal fisheries?

**RQ2:** How often does the intermediate advisory-scope restriction become binding relative to binary gating during historical replay of coastal operating conditions?

The contribution of this study is a domain-specific architecture that distinguishes AI participation from advisory scope across SAFE, CAUTION, and UNSAFE states. The study also provides preliminary historical evidence showing when the intermediate advisory restriction becomes active.

## 2. METHODOLOGY

The study follows a Design Science Research approach [4], involving architecture design, prototype implementation, retrospective technical evaluation, and planned contextual validation with small-scale coastal fishers. The present study reports the architecture and preliminary historical evaluation.

### 2.1 Architecture Design

The proposed architecture consists of four layers: environmental state representation, deterministic safety-state classification, state-gated AI advisory reasoning, and human decision-making.

The environmental state is represented as:

> **E = {w, r, m, o, v, t}**

where w is wind speed, r is rainfall intensity, m is marine-warning information, o is wave condition, v is vessel category, and t represents time-related operating conditions.

The environmental state is classified by:

> **S = f(E), S ∈ {SAFE, CAUTION, UNSAFE}**

Two controls are applied after classification. The participation gate G(S) determines whether AI is permitted to participate, while AAI(S) defines the advisory types available in each state.

**Table 1. Graduated safety-state gating**

| State | AI Participation | Permitted Advisory Set | Advisory Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAFE | Enabled | {Go, Delay, DepartureTime, Duration} | Full |
| CAUTION | Enabled | {Go, Delay} | Restricted |
| UNSAFE | Disabled | ∅ | None |

The permitted advisory spaces satisfy:

> **AAI(SAFE) ⊃ AAI(CAUTION) ⊃ AAI(UNSAFE) = ∅**

This separation is important because participation and recommendation scope represent different governance decisions. Under CAUTION, the AI remains available but cannot recommend DepartureTime or Duration. Under UNSAFE, no AI recommendation is permitted. The restriction is applied before advisory reasoning by exposing only the recommendation rules permitted for the classified state. Human decision authority is retained throughout the architecture.

### 2.2 Safety-State Classification

The deterministic classifier combines environmental warning criteria, vessel-related evidence, and explicitly defined design parameters.

Wind-state boundaries were informed by Malaysian strong-wind warning criteria, with transitions at 21.6 kn and 27.0 kn [5]. Rainfall-state transitions were set at 10 mm/h and 20 mm/h, informed respectively by the upper boundary of the DID light-rainfall category and the METMalaysia thunderstorm-warning rainfall criterion [6], [7]. The intermediate mapping between these source-informed boundaries is a researcher-defined design choice. These values are not interpreted as official fishing-safety limits.

Wave conditions are considered together with vessel category. Malaysian fishing-vessel classification and small-vessel seakeeping evidence are informed by Yaakob et al. [8], while vessel-dependent wave restrictions are additionally informed by Jeong and Im [9]. Because the available studies do not establish a complete SAFE-CAUTION-UNSAFE classification for the present study context, the resulting mappings are treated as domain-specific design parameters rather than universally validated safety thresholds.

Time is included as an operating variable using sunrise and sunset as maritime-relevant boundaries. COLREGs Rule 20(b) establishes sunset-to-sunrise requirements for navigation lights [10]; however, the use of this boundary for AI advisory restriction is a conservative governance design choice rather than a COLREGs requirement.

Marine-warning information is included in the architecture, but a suitable historical archive was unavailable for the retrospective study period. It was therefore excluded from the historical replay.

The classifier is evaluated as a deterministic governance mechanism using source-informed and explicitly declared design parameters.

### 2.3 Historical Evaluation

The historical evaluation compares the proposed graduated architecture against a binary-gated configuration.

Under binary gating, AI retains its full advisory capability whenever participation is enabled and provides no advice when the gate is closed. Under graduated gating, the intermediate CAUTION state retains AI participation while reducing the admissible recommendation set to {Go, Delay}.

This comparison allows the evaluation to focus specifically on whether the intermediate advisory restriction produces a meaningful difference beyond simple AI participation control.

Historical environmental data were obtained from ERA5 reanalysis through the Open-Meteo API [11], [12]. These data are distinct from the Malaysian sources used to inform selected classification boundaries.

The PRIMARY dataset covers January 2020 to December 2024 and contains 43,848 hourly reanalysis records, of which 9,135 fall within a researcher-defined 05:00–09:00 morning evaluation window. Because this window has not been validated against observed fisher departure behaviour, it is treated as an evaluation design parameter.

The primary evaluation asks whether graduated gating produces a smaller admissible advisory set than binary gating while AI participation remains enabled:

> **Δ = (N_restricted / N_eval) × 100%**

An alternative environmental-data configuration using finer-resolution wave reanalysis over its available 2021–2024 period was evaluated as a supporting robustness check. Because it differs from PRIMARY in both data characteristics and temporal coverage, it is not treated as an independent replication or statistical uncertainty estimate.

### 2.4 Planned Contextual Validation

A subsequent research phase will evaluate whether small-scale coastal fishers understand the SAFE, CAUTION, and UNSAFE states and the corresponding advisory restrictions. Human-participant data collection will commence only after the required institutional procedures, with voluntary informed consent and appropriate protection of participant data.

## 3. RESULTS & DISCUSSION

### 3.1 Preliminary Historical Results

Within the 9,135 PRIMARY evaluation-window records, graduated gating restricted the admissible advisory scope while retaining AI participation in 5.81% of records.

Under these conditions, binary gating retained the full advisory set, whereas graduated gating restricted recommendations to {Go, Delay} while retaining AI participation.

An alternative environmental-data configuration produced a corresponding result of 4.48%.

**Table 2. Preliminary historical evaluation**

| Configuration | Evaluated Records | Intermediate Advisory Restriction |
|---|---|---|
| PRIMARY | 9,135 | 5.81% |
| Alternative environmental-data configuration | 5,935 | 4.48% |

The 4.48% alternative result supports the presence of the same behaviour under a different data configuration, but the difference from 5.81% is not an uncertainty interval or an effect attributable solely to wave-data resolution because temporal coverage and other data characteristics also differ.

### 3.2 Discussion

The results illustrate the practical distinction between participation control and advisory-scope control. A binary gate determines whether AI should participate. The graduated architecture adds a second question: if AI participates, what should it be permitted to recommend?

For RQ1, the architecture achieves graduated control by separating AI participation, G(S), from the state-dependent advisory set, AAI(S). This allows advisory capability to be reduced under CAUTION without requiring complete AI withdrawal.

This distinction is most relevant under CAUTION. Instead of moving directly from full AI advisory capability to complete withdrawal, the architecture provides an intermediate response in which AI remains available but operates within a narrower advisory space.

The 5.81% result measures how often the additional restriction became relevant; it does not represent an improvement in safety, decision quality, or accident reduction. The evaluation concerns governance behaviour rather than downstream human or operational outcomes.

Several limitations should be recognised. The replay uses ERA5-based reanalysis rather than direct METMalaysia observations, while historical marine-warning information was unavailable. Some vessel-dependent wave mappings are adapted from studies outside Sabah and require local validation. The 05:00–09:00 evaluation window is also researcher-defined and has not yet been confirmed against observed fisher departure behaviour. These limitations restrict claims about operational effectiveness but do not prevent evaluation of the architecture's governance behaviour.

## 4. CONCLUSION

This study presents a graduated safety-state-gated architecture for AI decision support in low-resource coastal fisheries. The architecture separates AI participation from advisory scope across three deterministic operating states: SAFE permits full advisory capability, CAUTION retains AI participation with a restricted recommendation set, and UNSAFE withdraws AI advice.

Preliminary historical replay showed that the intermediate advisory restriction became active in 5.81% of PRIMARY evaluation-window records. An alternative environmental-data configuration produced a corresponding rate of 4.48%. These findings show that graduated advisory control creates a measurable governance distinction from binary gating under the evaluated historical conditions.

The results establish the behaviour of the proposed governance architecture rather than its real-world safety effectiveness. Future work will focus on contextual validation with small-scale coastal fishers, local calibration of operating parameters, and evaluation of how users interpret and respond to graduated AI advisory states.
