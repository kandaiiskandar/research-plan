## 2. Verified Facts (from the press release itself)

- **SmartCatch** won the **AI for Planet Prize** at the 2025 AI for Good Global Summit (United Nations, Geneva); one of three finalists from **330+ global entries** in the category.
- Uses **on-device AI** to identify fish **species, size and weight from a single mobile-phone image**; designed for coastal areas with scarce data and limited internet connectivity. Lead developer: Dr Hamza Altarturi (WorldFish postdoctoral fellow).
- Part of the **AI Small Scale Fisheries Sustainability Suite** (open-source): SmartCatch, AskData, EcoRoute (route planning to reduce fuel use and protect overfished reefs), built around **Peskas** (open-source monitoring platform, running since 2017 across Asia and Africa — the Longobardi et al. [20] system already in the references).
- **Deployment status — important:** the press release states SmartCatch and AskData are *"pilot projects with proof-of-concept prototypes, still under development and not yet deployed."*
- Key quote (Altarturi): *"SmartCatch is built for communities that are managing real climate risks with basic phones and no signal."*

## 4. How This Study Can Use It

**Role:** contemporary, internationally recognised example of fisheries AI in the exact deployment context of the thesis — low-resource, offline-first, basic phones, no connectivity — from a Malaysian-headquartered organization. Candidate location: conference paper §4.5 (fisheries and low-resource deployment); thesis Chapter 1/2 domain context.

**The useful contrast:** the entire award-winning suite targets **data collection, monitoring, and reporting** (catch identification, catch data, route planning). None of it is decision support for the departure decision, and none of it has any advisory governance concept — there is no advisory output to govern. This is consistent with the review's §4.5 finding (Haque & Al Jufaili [17]): fisheries AI is advancing on the data side while the governed-advisory dimension remains empty.

**Wording corrections vs. the suggested draft text (do not overclaim):**
- Do **not** write that SmartCatch "demonstrates that AI can be deployed effectively in resource-constrained environments" — per the press release it is a **proof-of-concept prototype, not yet deployed**. Correct claim: it demonstrates that on-device, low-resource fisheries AI is *technically feasible and institutionally recognised* (UN prize), with Peskas as the deployed component (already cited via Longobardi et al. [20]).
- Do **not** frame it as reinforcing the *advisory scope* gap directly — SmartCatch produces no recommendations, so it is evidence of the data-first orientation of fisheries AI, not of ungoverned advisory output.

**Suggested sentence (accurate version, if added to §4.5):**
> Recent recognition underscores this data-first orientation: WorldFish's SmartCatch — an on-device AI that identifies fish species, size, and weight from a single phone image in low-connectivity settings — won the AI for Planet Prize at the UN's 2025 AI for Good Global Summit, yet the tool and its companion suite target catch monitoring and reporting; decision support for the departure decision, let alone governed advisory output, remains absent from the domain's AI toolkit [WorldFish, 2025].
