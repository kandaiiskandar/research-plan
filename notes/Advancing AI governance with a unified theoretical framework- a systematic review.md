## 2. Verified Content (abstract only)

- **Problem:** Governments must regulate emergent AI while fostering innovation. Existing AI governance frameworks are numerous (**over 100**) but "focus narrowly on specific sectors, lack adaptability to evolving technologies, and fail to balance ethical standards with public accountability."
- **Method:** Systematic review identifying these gaps, particularly frameworks that prioritise either the public or the private sector.
- **Proposal:** The **Unified AI Governance Framework (UAIGF)** — "a unified, flexible model that integrates core and peripheral principles across sectors," adapting to sectoral needs and technological change.
- **Claimed contribution:** addresses "the fragmented efforts in AI governance" with "a cohesive and adaptable approach for future regulation."

## 5. Provisional Positioning

**Positioning paragraph (abstract-level, safe to use):** Robles and Mallinson (2025), in a systematic review for *Perspectives on Public Management and Governance*, find that although more than 100 AI governance frameworks exist, they are sector-narrow, slow to adapt to technological change, and fragmented between public- and private-sector orientations; their proposed Unified AI Governance Framework responds by integrating core and peripheral governance principles across sectors. This work operates at the institutional layer — specifying what principles governance should embody — and is complementary to the present research, which supplies the runtime layer: a formally enforced mechanism (G(S), A_AI(S)) determining how an AI decision-support system's advisory scope is governed under changing environmental conditions. The coexistence of abundant principle-level frameworks with an absent runtime advisory-governance mechanism is itself consistent with this review's finding that AI governance effort concentrates at layers other than the one where the operator's safety is decided.
