## Section 12 — Key Concepts and Definitions

- **Expert system**  
  A system that simulates human expert decision-making using rules and knowledge bases  

- **Rule-based system**  
  Uses IF–THEN rules for deterministic reasoning  

- **Inference engine**  
  Executes rules to derive conclusions  

- **Knowledge base**  
  Stores domain knowledge and rules  

- **Hybrid expert system**  
  Combines rule-based reasoning with machine learning or other AI methods  

---

## Section 13 — Limitations and Unsolved Problems

- Strong dependence on expert knowledge  
- Limited adaptability to new situations  
- Difficult knowledge acquisition and maintenance  
- Lack of integration with modern AI systems  
- No governance mechanism for controlling AI behaviour

## Section 16 — Relation to My Research and Positioning

### Alignment

This paper supports:

- continued relevance of **rule-based systems in industrial settings**  
- importance of:
  - interpretability  
  - consistency  
  - structured decision-making  

- emergence of **hybrid AI systems (rule + ML)**  

---

### Gap

The paper:

- reviews systems that **execute decisions**  
- but does not address:
  - **when AI should participate**  
  - **what AI should be allowed to recommend**  
  - **how decisions should be governed under risk**  

---

### Positioning

Industrial expert systems demonstrate that rule-based reasoning remains a reliable and interpretable approach for decision support in real-world environments. However, they lack a formal mechanism to govern AI participation and constrain decision outputs under varying risk conditions. This research extends expert systems into a governance architecture that explicitly controls both AI participation and its admissible recommendation space.
