## Section 2 — Core Contribution

### Problem Addressed

Modern AI systems:
- rely heavily on **data-driven learning**
- suffer from:
  - lack of interpretability  
  - limited transparency  
  - weak accountability in decision-making  

---

### Proposed Solution

The paper reinforces the use of:

> **Rule-based reasoning systems (expert systems)**

as a structured and interpretable approach to intelligent decision-making.

- Highlights applications in:
  - healthcare

## Section 12 — Key Concepts and Definitions

- **Rule-based reasoning:**  
  Application of predefined IF–THEN rules to derive conclusions  

- **Knowledge base:**  
  Repository of domain-specific rules  

- **Inference engine:**  
  Mechanism that applies rules to facts  

- **Forward chaining:**  
  Data-driven reasoning process  

- **Backward chaining:**  
  Goal-driven reasoning process

## Section 16 — Relation to My Research and Positioning

### Alignment

This paper supports:

- the **theoretical foundation** of rule-based systems  
- justification for:
  - deterministic decision logic  
  - production rule systems  
  - interpretable decision-making  

---

### Gap

The paper:
- treats rule-based reasoning as a **standalone system**  
- does not:
  - integrate with AI components  
  - control AI behaviour  
  - define governance under risk  

---

### Positioning

Rule-based reasoning provides a deterministic and interpretable foundation for decision-making. However, it is presented as a static reasoning mechanism without integration into modern hybrid AI systems or governance structures. This research extends rule-based reasoning into a dynamic governance layer that constrains AI participation and recommendation scope under risk.
