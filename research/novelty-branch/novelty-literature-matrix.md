# Novelty Literature Matrix — Recommendation-Level Evidential Admissibility

**Prepared:** 17 November 2026 · **Author:** Claude (Cowork session) · **Status:** exploratory research branch. Separate from frozen RQ1–RQ3; no canonical artefact modified.

**Search date:** all searches and fetches 17 November 2026.

**Reading-depth convention, applied strictly to every row.** **FULL** = the PDF was retrieved and its extracted text read. **PAGE** = the page was fetched but what was read was a summarising rendering, not raw text — weaker than FULL. **ABSTRACT** = abstract or landing page only. **RECORD** = title, authors, venue only. **PR** = press release only.

**Peer-review status is stated for every row.** The 2026 evidence-sufficiency literature is almost entirely un-peer-reviewed, which cuts both ways and is flagged where it matters.

**Excluded as sources.** Auto-generated aggregator sites surfaced repeatedly and were discarded without citation: `lacuna.tiptreesystems.com`, `pith.science`, `awesomepapers.io`, `sotaverified.org`, `mlanthology.org`, `paperswithcode.co`, `liner.com`, `emergentmind.com`, `wizwand`, `edukatesg.com`, `churchofspiralism.com`. One plausible-sounding title found only on such a site ("Level-Dependent Partial Abstention in Hierarchical Multi-label Learning") **could not be traced to any real venue and may not exist**; it is not cited.

## 1. The two named starting points — both verified to exist

### CAP (ACML 2025) — **EXISTS**

Sina Tayebati, Divake Kumar, Nastaran Darabi, Dinithi Jayasuriya, Theja Tulabandhula, Ranganath Krishnan, Amit Ranjan Trivedi. **"CAP: Conformalized Abstention Policies for Context-Adaptive Risk Management for LLMs and VLMs."** *Proceedings of the 17th Asian Conference on Machine Learning (ACML 2025)*, PMLR vol. 304, pp. 926–941. **Peer-reviewed.** No DOI. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v304/tayebati26a.html · **Read: FULL.**

*Citation-key oddity worth noting:* the paper carries © 2025 and is ACML 2025, but the PMLR key is `tayebati26a` and the volume page shows a 2026-04-06 publication date. **Cite as ACML 2025.**

CAP's own framing is a coverage/informativeness/utility trade-off, **not** a "safety-coverage frontier". The frontier phrasing likely comes from the group's related preprints arXiv:2502.06884 and arXiv:2502.07255 (both **RECORD** only).

### Zhu et al. (ICML 2026) on action-conditional guarantees — **EXISTS**

Zihan Zhu, Shayan Kiyani, George J. Pappas, Hamed Hassani. **"Conformal Risk-Averse Decision Making with Action Conditional Guarantee."** *Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2026)*, Seoul. PMLR vol. 306, pp. 167248–167276. **Peer-reviewed.** Method name **AC-RAC**. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/zhu26z.html · https://openreview.net/forum?id=B5RiXueKqb · poster https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65697 (Wed 8 July 2026) · preprint arXiv:2606.05551 · code https://github.com/Telvc/AC-RAC · **Read: FULL.**

Title, authors, venue and the action-conditional claim all match the brief's description exactly. **This is the single most relevant paper found, and it is the principal threat to the novelty claim.**

## 3. What the matrix shows, by axis

**Unit of abstention.** The literature partitions into: whole prediction (#21, #23, #24, #32), a label within one output space (#5), an interval width (#3, #29), a field or component (#2, #17, #19), a sub-claim or specificity rung (#4, #30), an output *mode* (#4), an action (#1, #15, #26), a verdict (#6, #7, #7b), and an entire recommendation category (#8, #9, #10, #12). **Category-level restriction exists and is not new.**

**Evidence versus confidence.** Confidence-driven: #1, #4, #5, #15, #26, #30, #32. Evidence-driven: #2 (provenance), #3 and #29 (missingness), #6 and #7 (logical insufficiency and conflict), #8 (lacking/poor-quality/conflicting), #14 and #21 (sufficiency independent of the model), #18 (staleness), #24 (conflict). Both: #17, #19, #25. **Evidence-driven admissibility exists and is not new.**

**Per-output-type requirements.** Present: #1 (per-action thresholds, uniform α), #2 (per-role α(r) — the full instance), #5 (per-class α_y, since 2019), #8 (per-grade certainty requirements), #9, #10, #12, #20, #7b. Absent: #3, #4, #14, #17 (uniform α), #19 (explicitly shared threshold), #22 (explicit negative). **Type-differentiated requirements exist and are not new.**

**Formal guarantees over evidence structure.** Only **#3 (Zaffran et al., ICML 2023)** has strong theorems indexed by evidence availability — and its output type never changes. **#6 and #7** have soundness, correctness and knowledge-monotonicity theorems over insufficiency, for a single property. **#1** has action-conditional coverage, but over confidence, not evidence. Everything else is empirical or procedural.

**Negative results that bear directly on the candidate problem.** #22 (peer-reviewed): availability-aware strategies change the model, never the output label set. #23: refusal is not a readout of visual evidence. #24 (unreviewed): a conflict/sufficiency-aware rule reached 90.05% selective accuracy at 53.58% coverage against **90.36% for a plain confidence margin at matched coverage** — the author's own framing is that the contribution is interpretability, not accuracy. **A committee will raise #24.**

## 4. What could not be verified

**Load-bearing and unread.**

1. **Ge Zhang et al., *Medicine Discovery* (#25) — read only via the AAAS EurekAlert press release (https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1147431).** It is the closest source in domain and framing to the candidate problem. Every statement attributed to it is the press release's characterisation, not the paper's text. No full author list, no volume confirmation, no confirmation of the journal's peer-review process. *Medicine Discovery* (ISSN 3117-9282) appears to be a very new Elsevier title — DOI suffix 100004 suggests roughly its fourth article. **This must be obtained and read before any novelty claim is finalised.** The `doi.org` redirect and two ScienceDirect PIIs were blocked; one of those PIIs was a guess and must not be cited.
2. **The ACC/AHA Class-of-Recommendation × Level-of-Evidence grid**, and GRADE's own verbatim strong/weak definitions. Both ACC/AHA pages returned client errors; `book.gradepro.org` serves JavaScript only and the Handbook truncated before §6.1.x. **The specific claim that evidence level constrains recommendation class is therefore unverified** — the GRADE-14 body text (which *was* read) defines strength without making that link. A strong lead, not an established citation.
3. **Belief-function theory** (Dempster–Shafer conflict and reliability discounting) was surfaced by search but **no primary source was read**. It plausibly contains closer prior art on conflict-and-reliability-conditioned decision admissibility than anything retrieved. **This is an acknowledged hole in coverage.**
4. **Active feature acquisition and test-selection primary sources** (Kachuee et al. NeurIPS 2018; arXiv:2502.11067 survey; Bilgic's thesis) — none opened. **Second acknowledged hole**, and directly relevant, since "acquire more evidence or decide now" is adjacent to the candidate problem.
5. **Age-of-information literature** surfaced but is about scheduling and control, not output admissibility; not pursued to primary sources.

**Venue and status unconfirmed.** #30, #33 (arXiv IDs only — do **not** cite as peer-reviewed); #7b (no venue or year in the PDF); #21 (widely associated with ICLR 2025, version fetched showed no venue field); Heng & Soh arXiv:2505.15008 (a search result rendered a header reading "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026"; the fetch did not return it — probable, unconfirmed). **Items #2, #10–#21, #24, #28, #29 are all arXiv or Research Square preprints with no venue listed.**

**Theorems not seen.** #11's soundness/completeness claim was read from a landing page; no proofs were read. #1's Appendix D (rare-action stress tests, action-space scaling) was read only in grep fragments — these are the most relevant robustness evidence if AC-RAC is to be contested.

**Truncated reads.** #15 (before experiments, so its baselines and results are unknown), #21, #22, and the GRADE Handbook.
